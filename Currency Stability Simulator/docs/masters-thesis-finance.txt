================================================================================
                              ABSTRACT
================================================================================

Financial stability in open emerging markets depends on the joint management of
foreign-exchange risk, official reserves, policy rates, and household asset
allocation between local and foreign currency. This thesis examines these
interactions through the lens of the Currency Stability Simulator—an open-
source, discrete-time financial–macro simulation built for interactive stress
testing and pedagogy.

From a finance perspective, the model treats the exchange rate as the key
relative price of local vs. foreign money, FX pressure as a composite risk
index, reserves and intervention as liquidity and defense capacity, and USD
usage as a measure of asset substitution (dollarization). Each monthly tick
updates market-facing variables: exchange rate path, inflation (real return
erosion), import cost index (corporate input shock), stability score (systemic
risk proxy), and a four-state regime label (Stable, Weak, Crisis, Collapse).

The thesis formalizes the financial interpretation of each module, maps them to
standard concepts (FX exposure, carry/support from interest differentials,
intervention exhaustion, flight-to-quality into USD), and presents scenario
analysis using five calibrated presets and three shock overlays (oil spike,
war/disruption, IMF financing). Comparative runs show that reserve adequacy
strongly conditions intervention effectiveness; that panic sensitivity acts as
a risk multiplier on FX demand; and that high USD preference creates structural
hedging demand that policy rates only partially offset.

The simulator is not a trading or forecasting system. Coefficients prioritize
feedback clarity over empirical calibration. Nonetheless, the framework offers
finance students and practitioners a compact laboratory for exploring how
balance-sheet buffers and behavioral flight interact before systemic crisis
thresholds are breached.

Keywords: financial stability, FX risk, reserve management, currency intervention,
dollarization, asset substitution, stress simulation, emerging markets

                          1. INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

1.1 Problem Statement

Corporate treasurers, sovereign debt managers, and central bank reserve desks
operate under coupled risks: spot FX moves affect imported input costs and
foreign-currency liabilities; inflation erodes nominal returns on local-currency
assets; households reallocate portfolios toward dollars when real returns turn
negative or policy credibility falters. Traditional finance curricula often
cover derivatives hedging, CAPM, and fixed-income analytics separately from
macro FX crises, leaving a gap in understanding how macro feedback translates
into sustained balance-sheet stress.

1.2 Objectives

This finance-oriented thesis aims to:

  O1. Reframe the Currency Stability Simulator in financial stability language.

  O2. Identify which parameters correspond to observable financial concepts
      (FX exposure, reserve months of imports, implicit carry, substitution).

  O3. Evaluate preset scenarios as stylized stress tests comparable to board-
      level scenario planning exercises.

  O4. Derive practical lessons for reserve adequacy, intervention strategy, and
      dollarization monitoring.

1.3 Research Questions

  RQ1: How does effective reserve strength govern the marginal efficacy of FX
       intervention in reducing depreciation pressure?

  RQ2: What combinations of USD preference and panic sensitivity produce rapid
       asset substitution despite moderate inflation?

  RQ3: Can interest-rate support stabilize the exchange rate without triggering
       secondary instability via import costs or fiscal stress (abstracted)?

  RQ4: How do external liquidity shocks (IMF inflow vs. sanctions/outflow) alter
       the path to Crisis or Collapse thresholds?

1.4 Relation to the Economics Thesis

A companion economics thesis emphasizes macro causal chains and policy credibility.
This document emphasizes balance-sheet capacity, risk indices, intervention as
 contingent liquidity deployment, and USD share as portfolio flight—complementary
views of the same simulation engine (src/engine/simulation.ts).

================================================================================
                       2. LITERATURE REVIEW
================================================================================

2.1 FX Risk and Corporate Exposure

Allayannis and Weston (2001) and Bartram, Brown, and Minton (2010) document
material FX exposure for multinational and import-dependent firms. The import
cost index in the simulator aggregates FX level and import dependency—a reduced-
form input-cost shock index finance managers can treat like a weighted commodity
FX beta.

2.2 Official Reserves and Crisis Prevention

Obstfeld, Shambaugh, and Taylor (2010) analyze reserve accumulation and crisis
frequency. Jeanne and Rancière (2011) discuss optimal reserves vs. insurance
costs. In the model, fxReserveStrength enters FX pressure relief, depreciation
support, intervention scaling, and stability scoring—mirroring the multi-use role
of reserves as liquidity backstop and confidence signal.

2.3 FX Intervention Effectiveness

Dominguez and Frankel (1993) and Adler et al. (2021) study when intervention
moves the spot rate vs. when it wastes ammunition. The simulator's intervention
term is multiplied by effective reserves, encoding diminishing credibility when
buffers are low—a stylized exhaustion curve without explicit option-market data.

2.4 Dollarization and Asset Substitution

Honohan and Shi (2002) survey deposit dollarization; Ize and Levy-Yeyati (2003)
analyze financial dedollarization policies. USD usage in the model is a one-
dimensional proxy for household and firm shift into foreign-currency assets
when local real returns and trust deteriorate.

2.5 Systemic Risk and Early Warning

Kaminsky, Lizondo, and Reinhart (1998) propose leading indicators of currency
crises (reserve losses, real exchange rate, credit growth). The stability score
and FX pressure index function as internal early-warning composites, analogous
to simplified market-based stress gauges used in macroprudential dashboards.

2.6 Stress Testing in Finance

Regulatory and institutional stress testing (BCBS, 2009; Fed CCAR framework)
uses scenario shocks on macro variables to evaluate loss absorption. The
simulator's presets and discrete shocks operationalize a lightweight, interactive
 analogue for classroom and workshop settings.

================================================================================
              3. FINANCIAL INTERPRETATION OF THE MODEL
================================================================================

3.1 Exchange Rate as Core Price

State variable exchangeRate measures local currency units per one US dollar.
Rising values imply depreciation—adverse for net importers and entities with
USD liabilities unhedged. Initial value: 100 (normalization, not a specific
country quote).

Monthly change derives from net depreciation:

  netDepreciation = max(0, pressureDepreciation − rateSupport − reserveSupport)

Finance reading: FX return on local currency is approximately negative net
depreciation; USD holders gain local-currency nominal wealth.

3.2 FX Pressure as Composite FX Demand Index

FX pressure (0–100) aggregates:

  Pull side (demand for USD):
    - Import dependency → trade invoicing and settlement need
    - Inflation → negative real return on local cash
    - USD preference → structural dollar bias
    - Panic sensitivity → multiplier on pull components

  Buffer side (supply / defense):
    - FX intervention × effective reserves
    - GDP strength → implicit external earning capacity
    - Reserve strength → stock ammunition
    - Trust → reduced precautionary USD demand

Finance reading: pressure resembles a net order-flow imbalance index before
central bank absorption.

3.3 Interest Rate as Carry and Stabilization Support

Higher interestRate adds rateSupport to reduce net depreciation and subtracts
from monthly inflation (disinflation channel). It slightly slows USD adoption
(higher local yield).

Finance reading: partial offset to FX weakness via interest differential
(carry attractiveness for local currency assets), subject to credibility limits
not modeled explicitly.

3.4 Reserves and Intervention

effectiveReserveStrength = clamp(fxReserveStrength + shock bonuses, 0, 100)

Intervention relief = (fxInterventionStrength / 100) × (reserves / 100) × 36

Reserve support to depreciation = (reserves / 100) × 0.0022 monthly

Finance reading: intervention is a contingent asset sale; effectiveness is
 convex in reserve stock—consistent with market perceptions that empty promises
 fail. Peg Collapse preset (high intervention, low reserves) demonstrates
 unsustainable defense.

3.5 Inflation as Real Return Erosion

Inflation is displayed annualized but built from monthly flows including pass-
through from depreciation on import basket:

  importInflationPass ∝ importDependency × erMonthlyPct × trust adjustment

Finance reading: unhedged import exposure creates a natural hedge shortfall
when FX and CPI co-move—typical emerging-market risk.

3.6 USD Usage as Dollarization / Asset Substitution

usdUsage rises with inflation, trust gap, USD preference, FX pressure; falls with
rates and trust. Stability score penalizes high USD share (−0.22 × usdUsage).

Finance reading: rising USD share signals shrinking local-currency monetary
base demand, harder default risk management in local currency, and potential
liability dollarization feedback (not separately modeled).

3.7 Import Cost Index

importCostIndex = (exchangeRate / 100) × effectiveImportDependency

Finance reading: stress index for COGS sensitivity—useful for discussing
operating leverage and margin compression under FX shock without full financial
statements.

3.8 Stability Score as Systemic Risk Proxy

  stability = 100 − 0.28×inflation − 0.22×usdUsage − 0.32×fxPressure
              + GDP/trust/reserve rewards

Bounded 0–100. Maps to board-level "traffic light" dashboards.

================================================================================
                 4. RISK METRICS AND REGIME CLASSIFICATION
================================================================================

4.1 Regime States

  STABLE    — inflation ≤ ~22%, stability ≥ ~58, FX pressure ≤ ~52
  WEAK      — intermediate deterioration
  CRISIS    — stability < ~32, or inflation > ~55%, or FX pressure > ~78
  COLLAPSE  — inflation > ~120%, or stability < ~12, or FX rate > 8× initial

Finance interpretation: Collapse corresponds to loss of local currency as unit
of account and store of value in model terms—analogous to market disorder and
forced restructuring episodes.

4.2 Monitoring Dashboard (Simulator UI)

  - Exchange rate time series → spot stress path
  - Inflation series → real return erosion
  - Currency mix pie → dollarization snapshot
  - Import cost index bars → corporate input shock
  - Stability gauge → composite risk
  - Status bar FX pressure → real-time pressure recomputation

4.3 History Window

Last 100 months retained—sufficient for medium-term stress path review
comparable to quarterly board risk reports over ~8 years of monthly data.

5.2 Parameters as Risk Appetite and Capacity Controls

Sliders (0–100) represent normalized policy and structure settings, not literal
percentages from national data. Finance users should treat them as stress-test
 dials:

  importDependency     → FX beta to trade channel
  fxReserveStrength    → liquidity war chest
  fxInterventionStrength → active defense intensity
  interestRate         → carry / tightening
  usdPreference        → structural hedge demand
  panicSensitivity     → market liquidity / herding multiplier

5.3 Shock Modules as Event Risk

  Oil price spike      — supply shock, import intensity ↑
  War / disruption     — trade + reserve drain narrative
  IMF bailout          — official sector liquidity injection

Shocks stack; durations count down one month per tick (aggregateModifiers).

5.4 Presets as Named Stress Scenarios

Defined in src/engine/presets.ts:

  Stable Economy         — base case for buffer calibration
  Emerging Market Stress — thinner liquidity, higher event risk
  Import Crisis          — high FX beta economy
  Hyperinflation Spiral  — fiscal dominance / monetization stress
  Peg Collapse           — intervention vs. reserve mismatch

5.5 Persistence

Snapshots saved to localStorage (STORAGE_KEY in types.ts) enable reproducible
workshop scenarios.

================================================================================
                  6. STRESS TESTING AND SCENARIO RESULTS
================================================================================

6.1 Baseline: Stable Economy

Financial profile: low FX beta (import 28), strong reserves (68), moderate
intervention, high trust. FX pressure remains subdued; exchange rate path mild;
USD share stable near high-20s percent; stability score > 70 typical at 24 months.

Lesson: adequate buffers make intervention optional rather than existential.

6.2 Emerging Market Stress

Reserves 38, printing elevated, panic 42. By month 12–18, FX pressure often
crosses Weak thresholds; import cost index trends up with spot; corporate margin
 stress narrative applies. Rate hikes (interest slider↑) reduce depreciation speed
 but may not restore Stable if reserves continue implicit drain via support term.

6.3 Import Crisis — FX Beta Dominance

Import dependency 78. Small depreciation produces large import cost index moves;
inflation pass-through reinforces FX pressure. Unhedged importers face nonlinear
 damage—consistent with RQ1/RQ2 on vulnerability of high-beta structures.

6.4 Hyperinflation Spiral — Real Return Collapse

Money printing 88, rates 22, trust 28. USD usage can exceed 60% before Collapse;
local-currency debt real value erodes rapidly in narrative terms. Demonstrates
 limits of local-currency financing when dollarization accelerates.

6.5 Peg Collapse — Intervention Exhaustion

Intervention 72 with reserves 32. Intervention relief term appears high in
isolation but reserve scaling caps effective defense; FX pressure persists;
 eventual sharp depreciation when netDepreciation remains positive month after
 month—resembles failed peg exits in emerging markets.

6.6 Shock Overlay Results

Starting Stable Economy at month 0:

  Shock          | Peak FX Pressure Effect      | Financial Channel
  ---------------|------------------------------|----------------------------------
  Oil (6 mo)     | Moderate import index spike  | Energy/import COGS shock
  War (8 mo)     | Strong + reserve penalty     | Sanctions/reserve runoff
  IMF (10 mo)   | Reserve boost, inflation ↓   | Official liquidity line

Combined war + pre-existing Emerging Market Stress preset often reaches Crisis
within shock window—illustrating inadequate self-insurance.

6.7 Interest Rate vs. Intervention Experiment (RQ3)

Hold Emerging Market Stress preset; compare:

  Case A: interestRate 55, intervention 40
  Case B: interestRate 35, intervention 70

Case B may show temporary pressure relief if reserves remain, but depletes
 implicit reserve support faster via sustained high intervention setting without
 replenishment (modeled only through shock modifiers). Case A slows inflation
 and USD flight via carry but allows more spot weakness. No universal dominant
 strategy—tradeoff between spot stability and real economy inflation/import cost.

================================================================================
            7. IMPLICATIONS FOR RESERVE AND RISK MANAGEMENT
================================================================================

7.1 Reserve Adequacy

Treat fxReserveStrength as a policy target linked—in real applications—to
imports coverage, short-term external debt, and M2 dollarizable deposits (Greenspan-
Guidotti rule extensions). Simulations suggest reserves affect outcomes through
 three distinct channels: direct pressure buffer, intervention scaling, and
 stability score. Underinvestment raises simultaneous probability of Crisis
 classification.

7.2 Hedging Strategy

Firms with import dependency analogous to slider > 60 should map simulator import
 cost index paths to hedge ratios on FX forwards/options. Pass-through to CPI
 in the model warns that spot-only hedging may be insufficient if pricing power
 is weak (trust low → higher pass-through multiplier).

7.3 Dollarization Monitoring

Track usdUsage like a deposit dollarization ratio. Thresholds above ~45–50% in
 stressed presets coincide with Weak/Crisis transitions—potential internal KPI
 for macroprudential review.

7.4 Intervention Policy

Use fxInterventionStrength together with reserve slider: aggressive intervention
 with low reserves scores poorly on sustained stability. Finance recommendation
 aligned with literature: intervene decisively early when buffers exist, or
 pre-communicate float/crawl exit to avoid middle strategies.

7.5 Official Sector Financing Shocks

IMF module shows liquidity can extend runway but does not eliminate structural
 FX beta. Conditionality narrative maps to requiring parameter improvement
 (lower import dependency, higher trust) for exit from Weak status.

================================================================================
                          8. LIMITATIONS
================================================================================

  - No term structure, forward points, or implied volatility
  - No sovereign spread or CDS channel
  - No explicit balance sheets for banks, firms, or government
  - USD-only foreign asset; no cryptocurrency or gold substitution
  - Linear-ish reduced forms; no fat-tail Monte Carlo
  - Parameters not tied to market quotes or IMF WEO data
  - Cannot price derivatives or compute hedge effectiveness numerically

Extensions for finance audience: add FX forward hedge slider reducing effective
 import pass-through; stochastic FX shocks; VaR/CVaR on import cost index;
 sovereign default trigger linked to Collapse state.

================================================================================
                          9. CONCLUSION
================================================================================

This finance thesis reinterpreted the Currency Stability Simulator as a compact
financial stability stress lab. Exchange rate dynamics, reserve-backed
intervention, interest-rate carry, and USD asset substitution jointly determine
whether the system remains Stable or escalates to Crisis and Collapse.

Key finance takeaways:

  1. Reserves are a multiplier on intervention credibility—not merely a stock
     for accounting.

  2. Import dependency acts as corporate and sovereign FX beta; high beta
     economies require larger hedging and liquidity buffers.

  3. Panic and USD preference convert moderate macro stress into accelerated
     portfolio substitution—an early-warning focus for risk officers.

  4. Official liquidity (IMF shock) buys time; structural parameters must move
     for sustained rating stability analogues.

The simulator complements—not replaces—empirical early-warning models and
market-based risk systems. Its value lies in making multi-channel FX–inflation–
dollarization feedback tangible for finance education and structured scenario
dialogues.
