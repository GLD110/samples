ABSTRACT
================================================================================

Financial stability in emerging markets hinges on the joint behavior of spot
FX, inflation-linked real returns, official reserve buffers, and private
dollarization of balance sheets. This thesis examines the "3D Currency
Stability Simulator," a browser-based financial macro prototype that updates
key risk indicators monthly from six policy and market-friction controls. The
system produces trajectories for exchange rate depreciation, inflation,
normalized FX reserves, USD share of economic activity, trust, FX pressure,
and a composite stability score suitable for risk dashboards and scenario
stress testing in education.

Unlike production risk engines, the model is deliberately reduced-form and
deterministic: it prioritizes transparent feedback loops over econometric
forecast accuracy. Finance contributions include (i) framing FX pressure as a
latent risk factor driving depreciation and reserve drawdown; (ii) linking
intervention intensity to reserve replenishment with state-dependent
effectiveness; (iii) modeling dollarization as an endogenous market share
process driven by inflation, preference, and panic; and (iv) mapping financial
stress to real-time 3D cues (liquidity-colored flows, network exposure lines,
crisis overlays) aligned with quantitative HUD metrics.

Five calibrated narrative scenarios span from stable macro-financial conditions
to currency collapse. The thesis discusses use in treasury training, sovereign
risk communication, and limitations relative to VaR, sovereign spread models,
and balance-sheet-based dollarization metrics.

Keywords: financial stability, FX risk, reserve management, dollarization,
intervention policy, stress scenarios, interactive risk visualization

1. INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

1.1 Problem Statement

Corporate treasurers, sovereign debt investors, and macro-prudential authorities
monitor overlapping indicators: spot and forward FX, inflation breakevens or
CPI prints, reserve cover, and signs of liability dollarization. During stress,
these series interact non-linearly—depreciation raises import prices, erodes
real local-currency returns, accelerates FX hedging and USD adoption, and
consumes central bank buffers. Static risk reports understate path-dependent
escalation.

1.2 Objectives

This thesis:

(O1) Formalizes the simulator as a multi-factor stress toy model for finance
     audiences.

(O2) Interprets each control slider as a financial policy or market friction.

(O3) Demonstrates scenario presets as narrative stress tests.

(O4) Articulates model risk, governance, and boundaries versus production
     systems.

1.3 Scope

The artifact is an educational and communication tool implemented in TypeScript
for client-side execution. It does not provide investment advice, regulatory
capital calculations, or calibrated sovereign default probabilities.

1.4 Outline

Chapters 2–5 develop financial interpretation; Chapter 6 covers visualization;
Chapter 7 implementation; Chapter 8 applications; Chapter 9 limitations.

2. FINANCIAL STABILITY AND FX RISK: LITERATURE REVIEW
================================================================================

2.1 Sovereign FX Risk and Reserve Buffers

Literature on reserve adequacy (IMF frameworks; Jeanne and Rancière, 2011)
stresses months-of-imports cover and short-term external debt ratios. The
simulator compresses these into a normalized reserve stock depleted by pressure
and large monthly depreciations, replenished by intervention—useful for intuition,
not for reserve targeting rules.

2.2 Official Intervention and Sterilization

FX intervention can stabilize spot rates but has fiscal and quasi-fiscal costs
(Adler et al., 2015). The model's fxIntervention control reduces pressure and
inflation modestly while adding reserves, with diminished real-world efficacy
when buffers are thin—mirroring market skepticism toward prolonged defense of
misaligned pegs.

2.3 Inflation and Real Returns

High inflation erodes real returns on local-currency instruments, shifting
portfolio demand toward USD assets (Fisher effect and currency substitution).
Money printing in the model maps to expansionary policy or monetization shocks.

2.4 Dollarization and Balance Sheets

Full dollarization literature (Honohan and Shi, 2002) distinguishes asset
substitution, liability dollarization, and payment dollarization. The simulator's
usdUsage aggregate proxies combined market share effects relevant for FX liquidity
risk: greater USD share implies higher sensitivity to offshore rate shocks and
potential mismatch if incomes remain local.

2.5 Stress Testing and Narrative Scenarios

Regulatory stress tests (BCBS principles) require transparent scenarios. The
preset library offers reproducible narrative paths (emerging stress, import
crisis, hyperinflation, collapse) analogous to simplified top-down scenarios
without bank-level granularity.

3. RISK FACTOR ARCHITECTURE AND DYNAMICS
================================================================================

3.1 Risk Factors and Observables

Primary factors:
  - FX pressure (latent composite)
  - Panic (behavioral amplification)
  - Inflation (price instability)
  - Exchange rate (spot depreciation vs anchor)
  - FX reserves (buffer)
  - USD usage (dollarization / market share)
  - Trust (credibility, affects flows)

Outputs for monitoring:
  - stabilityScore (0–100): composite financial stability index
  - gdpIndex: activity proxy for concomitant real-sector risk

3.2 FX Pressure as Composite Index

FX pressure loads positively on import dependency, inflation (capped contribution),
USD preference, and reserve weakness; negatively on intervention. In finance
terms, it resembles a parsimonious Z-score combining external vulnerability and
market structure biases toward hard currency.

3.3 Depreciation Process

Monthly depreciation intensity rises when pressure exceeds a neutral band (0.25)
and with panic. This resembles a reduced-form drift in spot FX under stress,
without explicit interest-rate parity or forward curve.

3.4 Inflation as Risk Driver

Inflation enters through:
  - direct money printing channel with acceleration in high-inflation states;
  - imported inflation via exchange rate pass-through;
  - panic-feedback consistent with unanchored expectations.

For fixed-income readers, sustained inflation paths imply negative real yields
on unindexed local bonds.

3.5 Stability Score as Composite Indicator

The stability score penalizes inflation, FX pressure, dollarization, and low
trust; rewards reserves. It can be treated as an internal heat index for
visualization and teaching, not a published FSI substitute.

================================================================================
4. INTERVENTION, RESERVES, AND POLICY SPACE
================================================================================

4.1 Reserve Drawdown Mechanics

Reserves decrease when fxPressure exceeds 0.28, modeling sustained defense or
import-related outflows. Additional drawdown triggers when monthly depreciation
exceeds roughly 8%, scaled by import dependency—capturing larger import bills and
potential capital flight in reduced form.

4.2 Intervention Replenishment

Positive intervention adds to reserves each month. Combined with pressure
reduction, this represents sterilized or partially sterilized intervention under
favorable conditions. When reserves are already low, pressure remains elevated—
an informal representation of diminishing marginal credibility.

4.3 Policy Frontier (Illustrative)

Finance trade-off: defending the currency (intervention up) depletes reserves
unless offset by inflows; allowing depreciation raises imported inflation and
dollarization. Classroom users can trace efficient-frontier intuition manually
by sweeping intervention versus outcomes.

4.4 FX Intervention Slider Interpretation

Maps to intensity of official spot market operations and related macro-prudential
FX measures. Does not distinguish spot versus swap intervention or NDF markets.

================================================================================
5. DOLLARIZATION AS MARKET SHARE AND BALANCE-SHEET RISK
================================================================================

5.1 Dynamics

USD usage increases with:
  - inflation (opportunity cost of holding local money),
  - usdPreference (structural dollarization),
  - panic (flight to quality).

It decreases with trustInLocal (credibility, deposit insurance perceptions,
anchored policy).

5.2 Financial Stability Implications

Rising usdUsage correlates in the model with higher fxPressure loading and lower
stabilityScore weights—consistent with concern that dollarization raises
macro-financial fragility even if it reduces inflation risk for individual agents.

5.3 Liquidity Visualization

Particle color mix (green local, blue USD) provides immediate market-share
feedback, analogous to watching rising offshore dollarization in payment data.

5.4 Limits

No separate tracking of FX loans, unhedged corporates, or deposit dollarization
ratios—extensions left to future balance-sheet modules.

6. VISUALIZATION FOR FINANCIAL RISK COMMUNICATION
================================================================================

6.1 Design Goals

- Synchronize quantitative HUD with perceptual risk cues.
- Encode network exposure (import lines) for supply-chain FX risk discussions.
- Use crisis fog/shake/distortion only when stability and inflation thresholds
  indicate severe stress—avoiding false alarms in benign regions.

6.2 Mapping Table (Finance View)

| Visual element      | Financial interpretation                          |
|---------------------|---------------------------------------------------|
| Building height     | Real activity / revenue proxy (gdpIndex)          |
| Building lean       | FX pressure / imbalance                           |
| Green particles     | Local currency liquidity flows                    |
| Blue particles      | USD liquidity dominance                           |
| Import lines        | Trade/exposure network thickness                  |
| Fog / shake         | Systemic stress regime                            |
| Sparklines          | Historical risk factor trends                     |

6.3 Communication Governance

Any public use should display disclaimers: illustrative coefficients, not
bank-specific or country-calibrated projections.

================================================================================
7. SOFTWARE IMPLEMENTATION AND SCENARIO LIBRARY
================================================================================

7.1 Stack

React + Vite front end; Zustand state; tickSimulation in simulation.ts;
React Three Fiber scene components (city, particles, import network, crisis
effects). Monthly tick via SimulationClock or manual step.

7.2 History and Analytics

History buffer supports sparklines for inflation, FX, USD adoption, stability—
minimal time-series infrastructure for classroom debriefs.

7.3 Scenario Presets (Stress Narratives)

1. Stable Economy — baseline risk appetite.
2. Emerging Market Stress — elevated FX and inflation vol.
3. Import Crisis — external shock with thin buffers.
4. Hyperinflation Spiral — monetization + panic feedback.
5. Currency Collapse — reserves near exhaustion, usdUsage ~ 94%.

Each preset sets controls and partial initial state for reproducible workshops.

7.4 Determinism

Paths are reproducible given seeds and controls—important for audit trails in
training exercises.

================================================================================
8. STRESS TESTING APPLICATIONS AND GOVERNANCE
================================================================================

8.1 Treasury Training

Participants adjust intervention and trust controls while watching reserve and
depreciation paths—learning buffer management under import shocks.

8.2 Sovereign Risk Seminars

Presets facilitate discussion of tail paths resembling historical EM crises
without claiming empirical fit.

8.3 Model Risk Management

Users must document:
  - illustrative nature of coefficients;
  - absence of stochastic shocks (optional extensions);
  - no credit event or banking sector module.

8.4 Ethical Use

Not for retail product marketing or automated trading signals.

================================================================================
9. LIMITATIONS, MODEL RISK, AND EXTENSIONS
================================================================================

9.1 Model Risk Register

| Risk                    | Description                                      |
|-------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| Parameter uncertainty   | Coefficients not econometrically estimated       |
| Omitted variables       | No rates, spreads, debt, or banks                |
| Time aggregation        | Monthly step masks intra-day FX volatility       |
| Determinism             | No shock distribution or confidence bands        |
| Dollarization aggregate | Single usdUsage vs decomposed balance sheets     |

9.2 Extensions

- Add stochastic FX and inflation shocks (Monte Carlo bands).
- Calibrate to country panels (reserves/imports, CPI, NEER).
- Introduce short-term external debt and rollover risk.
- Export CSV for comparison with market-implied vol surfaces.
- Integrate value-at-risk on illustrative FX exposures.

9.3 Validation Path

Backtest qualitative ordering only (e.g., collapse preset more severe than
stable); formal metrics (CRPS, diebold-mariano) inapplicable without data link.

================================================================================
10. CONCLUSION
================================================================================

From a finance perspective, the 3D Currency Stability Simulator is a
transparent, client-side stress narrative engine linking FX pressure, reserves,
dollarization, and inflation into coherent risk trajectories. Its value lies in
financial literacy, workshop stress scenarios, and communication—not in
replacing sovereign risk models or enterprise risk systems. When governed
appropriately, it makes path-dependent FX and stability trade-offs accessible;
future work should add stochastic elements, balance-sheet dollarization metrics,
and empirical calibration to market and official statistics.

APPENDIX A: VARIABLE DEFINITIONS FOR RISK REPORTING
================================================================================

EconomicState (monthly observables):
  inflation          — annualized price growth (decimal)
  exchangeRate       — spot units per USD anchor (1 = baseline)
  usdUsage           — USD market share [0,1]
  localUsage         — 1 - usdUsage
  fxReserves         — normalized reserve buffer [0,1]
  trust              — credibility / confidence [0,1]
  fxPressure         — composite FX stress [0,1]
  stabilityScore     — internal stability index [0,100]
  gdpIndex           — real-sector proxy [0,1]

SimulationControls (policy / friction inputs):
  importDependency   — trade exposure
  moneyPrinting      — monetization / liquidity shock
  fxIntervention     — official FX support intensity
  trustInLocal       — credibility support
  usdPreference      — structural USD bias
  panicSensitivity   — behavioral amplification

Default controls (see presets.ts): importDependency 0.28, moneyPrinting 0.12,
fxIntervention 0.45, trustInLocal 0.72, usdPreference 0.22, panicSensitivity 0.25.
