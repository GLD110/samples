--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. PURPOSE OF THIS DOCUMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Department-oriented template for a finance master's thesis using the US vs
China banking simulation. Finance programs typically expect stronger quantitative
rigor, explicit risk and stability metrics, and clearer links to banking and
credit markets than a pure macro-institutions narrative.

Finance emphasis: credit supply, deposit funding, crisis transmission,
sensitivity and scenario analysis, optional calibration to banking aggregates.
The dashboard is a communication tool; examiners focus on model specification,
metrics, and robustness.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. PROPOSED TITLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Credit and deposit dynamics under alternative regulatory constraints: a
   comparative simulation of rate-based vs reserve-based banking regimes

2. Financial stability metrics in a dual-regime credit model: deposit shocks and
   policy modes in stylized US and China banking

3. Transmission of funding shocks to bank lending: reduced-form experiments with
   interest-rate and reserve-requirement wedges

Subtitle (recommended): Deterministic scenario analysis with reproducible
implementation

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. RESEARCH QUESTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Select two or three primary questions.

RQ1  How do lending capacity and credit utilization (C/K) differ between a
     rate-constrained and an RRR-constrained regime for comparable tightness of pi?

RQ2  What is the impact of a deposit shock on credit paths, and which regime
     shows shorter recovery time and lower maximum drawdown in credit?

RQ3  How sensitive are credit volatility and terminal credit to adjustment
     speeds alpha and money-creation parameter mu?

RQ4  Under recession vs stimulus modes, which regime benefits more in deposit
     replenishment and credit restoration after a shock?

RQ5  (optional) Can bounded calibration of (i, rho, alpha, mu) match selected
     moments of US and China bank credit growth (volatility, persistence)?

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. CONTRIBUTIONS (FINANCE FRAMING)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Comparative banking reduced form — Two policy wedges affecting loanable
   capacity from deposits.

2. Stability-oriented metrics — Drawdown, recovery time, credit–deposit ratios,
   divergence under common shocks.

3. Reproducible scenario engine — Documented parameters, batch replication,
   appendix code.

4. (Optional) Moment matching with publicly available credit series.

Explicit non-claims: alpha generation, optimal monetary policy, bank equity
pricing, or Basel capital — unless you extend the model.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. ABSTRACT TEMPLATE (150–300 WORDS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Bank credit and deposit funding interact with policy constraints that differ
    across financial systems. This thesis specifies a discrete-time model in
    which lending capacity equals deposits scaled by (1-pi), with pi as an
    interest-rate wedge (US) or reserve requirement ratio (China). Credit
    converges toward capacity at regime-specific speeds; lending growth feeds
    deposit creation. I simulate [N] policy and crisis scenarios, including a
    synchronized 20% deposit shock, recession mode (slower adjustment), and
    stimulus mode (stronger deposit feedback). Financial-stability metrics include
    credit drawdown, recovery horizons, and sensitivity to key parameters. Results
    indicate [finding 1] and [finding 2]. The model is illustrative and
    deterministic; [optional: moment targets met within bands X–Y]. The
    contribution is a transparent laboratory for comparative credit–deposit
    dynamics rather than a forecasting system for macro aggregates.

6. CHAPTER STRUCTURE (FINANCE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Typical length: 50–80 pages plus appendices (confirm: MFin / MSc Finance / MQF).

CHAPTER 1 — INTRODUCTION (6–10 pages)
CHAPTER 2 — LITERATURE REVIEW (10–16 pages)
  Blocks: bank lending channel; deposits and funding; reserve requirements;
  interest rates and margins; financial stability; simulation in finance education.

CHAPTER 3 — FINANCIAL AND REGULATORY CONTEXT (8–12 pages)
  Mapping table: instrument -> pi; omissions (capital, NPLs, interbank market).

CHAPTER 4 — MODEL SPECIFICATION (10–14 pages)

  4.1 Balance-sheet intuition
  Deposits D fund capacity; Credit C <= K; expansion feeds D via mu.

  4.2 Equations (aligned with code)
    K_t = D_t * (1 - pi_j) * phi
    C_t = C_{t-1} + alpha_j * (K_t - C_{t-1})
    D_t = D_{t-1} + mu * max(0, C_t - C_{t-1})
  Shock: D <- 0.8 * D at t = t*.  Output proxy: Y_t = 1.2 * C_t.
  Code: src/simulation/bankingModel.ts

  4.3 Parameter domains
    i     [0, 0.20]   US wedge
    rho   [0, 0.50]   China RRR stylization
    D_0   [1000, 10000]
    Steps [10, 100]

  4.4 Optional extensions: NPL drag on alpha; stochastic shocks; utilization u = C/K

CHAPTER 5 — DATA AND CALIBRATION (optional but recommended)
CHAPTER 6 — METHODOLOGY: SCENARIOS AND METRICS (8–12 pages)
CHAPTER 7 — RESULTS (12–16 pages)
CHAPTER 8 — ROBUSTNESS AND DISCUSSION (6–10 pages)
CHAPTER 9 — CONCLUSION (4–6 pages)
REFERENCES + APPENDICES A–D

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.2 FINANCIAL METRICS (DEFINE IN THESIS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Credit drawdown     (C_max - C_min) / C_max after shock
Recovery time       First t with C_t >= 0.9 * C_{t*-1}
Credit–deposit      C_t / D_t
Utilization         C_t / K_t
Regime divergence   |C_t^US - C_t^CN| at T
Path volatility     sum |C_t - C_{t-1}| or std of Delta log C

7. HYPOTHESES (FINANCE-ORIENTED)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

H1  Tighter pi lowers utilization and steady-state credit.
H2  Deposit shock produces larger drawdown when alpha is small.
H3  China stylization (higher alpha) reaches capacity faster after policy loosening.
H4  Stimulus mode shortens recovery time after shock.

9. EMPIRICAL / CALIBRATION APPENDIX (FINANCE TRACK)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Download US/China bank credit series.
2. Compute growth, vol, AR(1).
3. Choose (i, rho) so model credit-growth vol lies in [q25, q75] of data.
4. Figure: data vs model growth distributions (not forecast).
5. State: calibration to moments, not structural estimation.
