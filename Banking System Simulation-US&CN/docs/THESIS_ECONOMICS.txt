1. PURPOSE OF THIS DOCUMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This file is a department-oriented template for an economics master's thesis
built on the side-by-side US (interest-rate constraint) vs China
(reserve-requirement constraint) simulation. Adapt section titles and page
targets to your university's regulations and your supervisor's preferences.

Economics emphasis: institutions, monetary transmission, comparative macro,
policy trade-offs, and (optionally) time-series evidence. The web app supports
transparency; the thesis argument lives in equations, scenarios, and
interpretation.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. PROPOSED TITLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Choose one direction and refine with your supervisor.

1. Monetary transmission through credit and deposits: a stylized comparison
   of interest-rate and reserve-requirement regimes

2. Comparative dynamics of bank credit under alternative policy instruments —
   US and China in a reduced-form simulation

3. Deposit shocks and policy constraints: ceteris paribus experiments in a
   dual-regime banking model

Subtitle (recommended): A discrete-time simulation with policy and crisis
scenarios

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. RESEARCH QUESTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Select two or three; the rest can be secondary or appendix.

RQ1  How do lending capacity, credit, and deposits evolve when the policy
     constraint takes the form of an interest rate (US stylization) versus a
     reserve requirement ratio (China stylization), holding initial deposits
     and shock timing equal?

RQ2  After a common deposit shock (-20% at mid-horizon), which regime exhibits
     faster credit recovery and larger cumulative deposit loss, and how does
     that depend on adjustment speeds?

RQ3  How do recession mode (slower credit adjustment) and stimulus mode
     (stronger deposit creation from credit growth) alter comparative outcomes?

RQ4  (optional, empirical) Do impulse-like responses from the model align
     qualitatively with VAR or descriptive evidence on credit and monetary
     aggregates in the US and China?

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. CONTRIBUTIONS (WHAT YOU CLAIM)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

State these explicitly in the introduction; defend them in the conclusion.

1. Comparative institutional mapping — Links real US and China policy
   toolkits to a single reduced-form constraint pi in capacity K_t = D_t(1-pi).

2. Transparent reproducible model — Full discrete-time specification, parameter
   table, and open implementation (appendix + code).

3. Systematic scenario analysis — Grid over policy levels, shock on/off,
   recession/stimulus modes, with comparable metrics across regimes.

4. (If RQ4 included) Stylized facts / weak validation — Narrative or
   statistical alignment with data moments, not structural estimation of the
   full model.

What you do not claim: point forecasts of GDP, proof of welfare dominance of
one system, or causal identification of country differences from the toy model
alone.

5. ABSTRACT TEMPLATE (150–300 WORDS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    This thesis develops a stylized discrete-time model of bank credit and
    deposits under two policy constraints: an interest-rate wedge (US) and a
    reserve-requirement ratio (China). Lending capacity scales with deposits
    and the constraint; credit adjusts partially toward capacity each period,
    with faster adjustment in the China stylization; credit growth feeds
    deposit creation. A synchronized deposit shock and optional recession and
    stimulus modes generate comparative dynamic paths for credit, deposits, and
    a GDP proxy (credit x 1.2). Using [N] simulation scenarios and sensitivity
    analysis over [parameters], I show that [main finding 1] and [main finding
    2]. The results illustrate how instrument choice and adjustment speeds
    shape transmission after shocks, within the limits of a pedagogical reduced
    form. [Optional: Comparison with stylized facts from US/China credit series
    suggests qualitative consistency with …] The thesis contributes a
    reproducible framework for teaching and policy intuition, not empirical
    forecasting.

6. CHAPTER STRUCTURE (ECONOMICS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Typical length: 60–90 pages (excluding appendices); confirm locally.

CHAPTER 1 — INTRODUCTION (8–12 pages)
  - Motivation: different monetary architectures (price vs quantity levers;
    US vs China).
  - Research questions and hypotheses (e.g. faster adjustment implies sharper
    post-shock credit paths).
  - Contributions and structure of the thesis.
  - Limitations (reduced form, parallel shocks, GDP proxy).

CHAPTER 2 — LITERATURE REVIEW (12–18 pages)
  Organize by theme, not alphabetically.

  Theme                          | Example topics
  -------------------------------|----------------------------------------------
  Credit channel                 | Bernanke–Gertler; bank lending channel
  Money and deposits             | Money multiplier; endogenous money
  Reserve requirements           | PBoC RRR; liquidity management in China
  US monetary policy             | Federal funds to bank rates to lending
  Comparative central banking    | Tool mix; state-owned banks (China)
  Simulation / teaching models   | ABM and reduced-form macro pedagogy

  End with a gap paragraph: need for a transparent comparative experiment with
  common shock and metrics.

CHAPTER 3 — INSTITUTIONAL AND POLICY BACKGROUND (10–14 pages)
  - United States: policy rate, transmission to bank lending and deposits.
  - China: RRR, other tools (window guidance, MLF), credit and deposit aggregates.
  - Mapping to the model: justify pi_US = i, pi_CN = rho; discuss omissions
    (FX, capital controls, shadow banking, fiscal dominance).
  - Table: real instruments <-> model parameters.

CHAPTER 4 — THEORETICAL MODEL (12–16 pages)

  4.1 Setup
  Two parallel systems j in {US, CN}, discrete time t = 0,...,T-1.

  State variables: deposits D_t, credit C_t.

  Policy constraint: pi_US = i in [0, 0.2], pi_CN = rho in [0, 0.5].

  Capacity (stimulus boost phi in {1, 1.04}):
    K_t = D_t * (1 - pi_j) * phi

  Credit adjustment (alpha_US, alpha_CN depend on recession mode):
    C_t = C_{t-1} + alpha_j * (K_t - C_{t-1})

  Deposit evolution (mu in {0.52, 0.82} with stimulus):
    D_t = D_{t-1} + mu * max(0, C_t - C_{t-1})

  Deposit shock at step t*: D <- 0.8 * D (both systems).

  GDP proxy: Y_t = kappa * C_t, kappa = 1.2

  Implementation: src/simulation/bankingModel.ts (runSimulation)

  4.2 Baseline parameters (from code)

  Symbol      | Normal US | Normal CN | Recession | Stimulus
  ------------|-----------|-----------|-----------|----------
  alpha_US    | 0.18      | —         | 0.08      | —
  alpha_CN    | —         | 0.34      | 0.14      | —
  mu          | 0.52      | 0.52      | 0.52      | 0.82
  phi         | 1         | 1         | 1         | 1.04

  4.3 Comparative statics and steady-state discussion
  - Effect of raising i or rho on capacity and long-run credit.
  - Role of alpha (speed) vs pi (level constraint).

CHAPTER 5 — METHODOLOGY AND EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN (8–12 pages)
  - Deterministic simulation (no stochastic shocks unless extended).
  - Scenario matrix (minimum 12 runs — see Section 8).
  - Metrics: peak-to-trough credit; steps to 90% recovery; cumulative deposit
    change; divergence US–CN at horizon T.
  - Sensitivity: heatmaps over alpha, mu, and policy levels.
  - Replication: batch runs in Python/R mirroring bankingModel.ts.

CHAPTER 6 — RESULTS (12–18 pages)
  - Baseline paths (credit, deposits, GDP proxy).
  - Shock experiment (mid-horizon -20% deposits).
  - Recession vs stimulus modes.
  - Sensitivity tables and robustness.
  - Interpretation: regime comparison under shared assumptions (not causal
    country claims).

CHAPTER 7 — OPTIONAL EMPIRICAL STYLIZED FACTS (8–14 pages)
  Include if your program expects econometrics.
  - Data: FRED (US); China TSF or bank loans (official statistics).
  - Descriptive: growth, volatility, autocorrelation.
  - Optional: bivariate VAR — compare impulse direction to model.
  - Model is not full structural MLE estimation.

CHAPTER 8 — DISCUSSION AND POLICY IMPLICATIONS (6–10 pages)
CHAPTER 9 — CONCLUSION (4–6 pages)
REFERENCES
APPENDICES A–D (equations, scenarios, code, ethics forms)

H3  Deposit shock reduces credit more when alpha is low (recession mode).
H4  Stimulus mode raises deposit growth for a given credit path.
