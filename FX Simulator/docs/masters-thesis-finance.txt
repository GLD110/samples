1. ABSTRACT (draft, ~250 words)
================================================================================

International finance students must interpret bilateral FX quotes, link macro
and risk inputs to directional moves, and distinguish durable re-pricing from
short-term volatility. Yet many classroom tools either show static formulas or
live charts without attributing why the rate changed. This thesis presents the
FX Simulator, a client-side web application that computes a transparent
fundamental rate from five country-level inputs (rates, inflation, growth,
trade, risk) and overlays a bounded stochastic noise factor to mimic spot
deviation from fundamentals. The interface exposes contribution bars, ranked
"why did it change?" narratives, and scenario presets (hawkish policy, risk-off,
stagflation, export boom).

I evaluate whether this design improves financial literacy outcomes: correct
interpretation of A/B quoting, sign of P&L-relevant moves, ranking of dominant
drivers, and ability to explain the gap between fundamental and displayed spot.
A lab-based study compares simulator users against a control group using
traditional lecture notes plus static Bloomberg-style screenshots. The thesis
does not claim the heuristic linear model is empirically calibrated; instead it
contributes to finance pedagogy and explainable presentation of multi-factor
FX—a skill relevant to treasury, corporate FX hedging education, and retail
investor protection narratives.

Keywords: foreign exchange, financial education, market microstructure intuition,
attribution, fundamental vs spot, interactive learning

2. MOTIVATION AND RESEARCH QUESTIONS
================================================================================

2.1 Motivation
- FX is a core MSc Finance topic but students confuse quote conventions (direct/
  indirect), percentage changes, and macro news interpretation.
- Practitioners use dashboards; graduates often lack training in decomposing moves.
- Regulators and industry bodies stress understandable client communication;
  attribution UIs are understudied in finance curricula.

2.2 Main research question (RQ1)
After a structured introduction to bilateral FX, do students using the FX Simulator
score higher on tasks requiring (i) quote interpretation, (ii) driver attribution,
and (iii) fundamental–spot separation than students using static materials?

2.3 Secondary questions
RQ2: Does exposure to contribution bars reduce errors in identifying the largest
      driver in multi-shock presets?
RQ3: Does explicit "noise" visualization reduce the belief that all spot moves
      are explained by the five sliders?
RQ4: Do students report higher confidence in explaining FX moves to a non-expert
      (proxy for workplace communication)?

2.4 Non-goals (explicit)
- No claim of profitable trading strategy
- No high-frequency or order-book microstructure model
- No replacement for econometric exchange-rate forecasting literature

4. THE FX SIMULATOR — FINANCE-ORIENTED DESCRIPTION
================================================================================

4.1 Instrument definition
- Pair: A/B
- Fundamental F = 1 + sum_i w_i * (input_i,A - input_i,B)
- Spot display S = F * (1 + epsilon), epsilon from bounded random walk

4.2 Weights (heuristic, for classroom comparative statics)
  w_rate = +0.4, w_inflation = -0.3, w_gdp = +0.2,
  w_trade = +0.1, w_risk = -0.2

4.3 UI features relevant to finance thesis
- Hero: animated spot, arrow vs previous tick, fundamental line, noise %
- Presets as stress scenarios for classroom discussion
- Contribution panel: additive decomposition relative to parity anchor "1"
- Explanation panel: sorted by impact magnitude

4.4 Link to finance learning outcomes
| Learning outcome              | Simulator feature              |
|-------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Read a bilateral quote        | A/B headline, 4 decimal places   |
| Direction of appreciation     | Stronger/weaker country highlight|
| Multi-factor attribution      | Bars + ranked explanations       |
| Volatility vs fundamentals    | Noise timer vs slider-only F     |
| Scenario analysis             | Preset buttons                   |

5. METHODOLOGY
================================================================================

5.1 Study design
- Preferred: randomized control in MSc International Finance tutorial
- Alternative: two-cohort quasi-experiment (Year t vs t+1 with new tool)

5.2 Sample
- Finance MSc or senior BFin students with basic macro prerequisite
- Target N: 50–120; justify with power analysis on quiz proportion correct

5.3 Assessment battery (finance-tailored)
Section A — Quote mechanics
  - Convert move in A/B to % appreciation of A vs B
  - Identify base/quote in verbal scenarios

Section B — Attribution
  - Given slider positions, predict sign of change in A/B
  - Rank three largest contributions in a preset

Section C — Fundamental vs spot
  - Explain why spot differs from fundamental with noise only
  - True/false: "Moving inflation slider always explains today's tick"

Section D — Communication (short answer)
  - Write 150 words explaining a preset to a corporate treasurer

5.4 Treatment protocol (60–90 min)
- Control: PDF + static chart images + worked examples
- Treatment: guided tour of simulator + free exploration + preset tasks

5.5 Data and ethics
- Store anonymized scores; no personal trading data
- Clear disclaimer: educational toy, not investment advice

5.6 Econometrics of learning (simple)
- OLS: PostScore = alpha + beta*Treatment + gamma*PreScore + controls
- Logit for binary misconception indicators
- Report effect sizes (Cohen's d) for finance education audience
