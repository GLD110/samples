1. ABSTRACT (draft, ~250 words)
================================================================================

Undergraduate and early graduate courses in open-economy macroeconomics often
present exchange-rate determinants—interest differentials, inflation, growth,
external balances, and country risk—as separate channels. Students frequently
confuse relative levels with marginal effects and struggle to see how multiple
shocks combine in one quoted rate. This thesis develops and evaluates an
open-source, browser-based "FX Simulator" that maps country-level macro
sliders into a transparent linear fundamental rate and separates fundamental
valuation from a bounded noise layer mimicking short-run spot volatility.
Contribution bars and ranked natural-language explanations attribute changes
in the quote to each macro gap.

Using a between-subjects or within-subjects design, I compare learning
outcomes for students who study the same material with the simulator versus
a static slide deck or textbook excerpt. Primary outcomes are comprehension
(quiz scores on sign and magnitude of effects), calibration (which shock
dominates in preset scenarios), and self-reported confidence. Secondary
outcomes include time-on-task and qualitative feedback from think-aloud
sessions. The economic content of the tool is explicitly a teaching toy: fixed
heuristic weights, not estimated macro coefficients. The contribution is
therefore pedagogical and communicative—how to teach multi-factor FX
determination—not a claim of econometric fit to market data.

Keywords: exchange rates, open-economy macro, economic education, interactive
visualization, attribution, comparative statics

2. MOTIVATION AND RESEARCH QUESTIONS
================================================================================

2.1 Motivation
- Exchange-rate chapters bundle UIP-related intuition, PPP, Taylor-rule
  narratives, balance-of-payments stories, and risk premia; textbooks rarely
  let learners manipulate all channels in one screen.
- Post-COVID and high-inflation periods renewed student interest in "why is
  the currency moving?" but classroom tools are often static.
- Policy communication (central banks, treasuries) increasingly uses
  dashboards; economics education can mirror transparent decomposition.

2.2 Main research question (RQ1)
Does using an interactive FX simulator with explicit contribution attribution
improve student understanding of how macro differentials affect a bilateral
quote, relative to traditional static materials?

2.3 Secondary questions
RQ2: Which misconceptions (e.g. conflating spot noise with fundamentals,
      wrong sign on inflation differential) are most reduced?
RQ3: Do students correctly rank shock importance in preset scenarios (hawkish
      central bank, stagflation, risk-off) after treatment?
RQ4: Does separating "fundamental" from "noise" improve distinction between
      medium-run macro drivers and short-run volatility?

2.4 Scope limits (state clearly in thesis)
- The simulator's linear structure is a deliberate simplification, not a
  reduced-form estimate from panel data.
- Results apply to learning, not to trading or policy forecasting performance.

- Growth and productivity narratives; current account / trade balance

- Prior use of simulations in economics education (PhET-style, classroom
  experiments, macro sims)

3.3 Visualization, explainability, and learning sciences
- Decomposition charts and causal attribution in dashboards
- Cognitive load: when interactivity helps vs overwhelms

3.4 Gap
Few studies combine (i) multi-factor FX comparative statics, (ii) live
decomposition of the quote into additive contributions, and (iii) measured
learning gains in a randomized or quasi-experimental design.

4. THE FX SIMULATOR — ECONOMIC SPECIFICATION
================================================================================

4.1 Quote convention
- A/B = units of B per one unit of A (toy convention, stated in UI)

4.2 Fundamental (structural) rate
  A/B = 1
    + 0.4 * Delta(interest rate)
    - 0.3 * Delta(inflation)
    + 0.2 * Delta(GDP growth)
    + 0.1 * Delta(trade balance)
    - 0.2 * Delta(risk score)

  where Delta(x) = x_A - x_B for each input.

4.3 Spot display
  spot = fundamental * (1 + noise)
  noise: bounded random walk (~ +/-4%), not slider-driven

4.4 Pedagogical presets
- Balanced (fundamental = 1)
- Hawkish central bank in A
- Risk-off shock in B
- Stagflation in A
- Export boom in A

4.5 Mapping to course content (for thesis narrative)
| Simulator input      | Typical lecture link                    |
|----------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| Interest rate gap    | UIP / policy rate differential          |
| Inflation gap        | PPP / real appreciation-depreciation  |
| GDP growth gap       | Balassa-Samuelson / demand stories      |
| Trade balance gap    | External adjustment, CA shorthand     |
| Risk score gap       | Risk premium, sovereign stress          |

4.6 Why weights are heuristics
Discuss alignment with textbook signs without claiming calibration; optional
sensitivity appendix: re-run learning study with alternative weight sets.

5. METHODOLOGY
================================================================================

5.1 Design options (choose one with supervisor)
A) Randomized controlled trial: treatment = simulator lab; control = PDF/slides
B) Within-subjects: pre-test, simulator session, post-test
C) Quasi-experiment across two tutorial groups / universities

5.2 Participants
- Target: N >= 60–100 for adequate power on quiz scores (power analysis in
  appendix); economics undergraduates or MSc conversion students
- Ethics: informed consent, debrief, no real money or investment advice

5.3 Instruments
- Pre/post multiple-choice and short-answer (sign, magnitude, ranking shocks)
- Scenario tasks: "Given preset X, which country is stronger and why?"
- Optional: think-aloud (n=8–12) for qualitative chapter
- Survey: confidence, usefulness, cognitive load (NASA-TLX short form)

5.4 Procedure (90-minute lab example)
1. Pre-test (15 min)
2. Treatment or control material (35 min)
3. Post-test (15 min)
4. Questionnaire (10 min)
5. Debrief (5 min)

5.5 Analysis
- Primary: difference in post scores (ANCOVA with pre-score) or gain scores
- Secondary: item-level misconception rates; ranking accuracy on presets
- Qualitative: thematic coding of think-aloud transcripts

5.6 Threats to validity
- Hawthorne effect; instructor enthusiasm; prior macro exposure
- Mitigation: blind grading of open answers; standardized scripts

11. ONE-PARAGRAPH ELEVATOR PITCH (for supervisor meeting)

I build a browser-based FX teaching simulator that shows how five macro gaps
add up to a fundamental exchange rate and how random noise moves spot away from
that fundamental. My thesis tests whether this interactive decomposition helps
economics students understand multi-factor exchange-rate determination better
than static slides, using a controlled lab study and pre/post assessments—not
whether the model forecasts real markets.
