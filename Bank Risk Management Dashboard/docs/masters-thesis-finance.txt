================================================================================
                              ABSTRACT
================================================================================

Financial institutions measure and manage credit risk, liquidity risk, and
market risk (including interest-rate risk) under regulatory and internal
frameworks. Expected loss models decompose exposure into probability of
default, loss given default, and exposure at default; liquidity metrics monitor
funding stability; duration-based approximations quantify rate sensitivity of
fixed-income and loan-like positions. Stress testing applies adverse scenarios
to these measures before losses materialize.

This thesis documents the Bank Risk Management Dashboard, a browser-based
simulation that implements transparent formulas for all three risk types,
combines them into aggregate exposure and a heuristic risk score, and supports
stylized stress modes (recession, bank run, rate shock). A complementary AI
Credit Default Engine—implemented without external machine-learning libraries—
maps borrower and loan features to segment-level probabilities of default via
a weighted linear score and logistic link, then aggregates portfolio expected
default volume, AI expected loss, and a toy risk-weighted asset measure.

The contribution is pedagogical and methodological: full mathematical
specification aligned with source code, illustrative numerical validation,
discussion of model risk and limitations relative to Basel and industry
practice, and architecture suitable for finance education and prototype
risk-reporting workflows. The system is not a substitute for regulatory capital
engines or enterprise risk platforms.

Keywords: bank risk management; expected loss; PD; LGD; EAD; liquidity stress;
duration; interest-rate risk; stress testing; scorecard; logistic regression;
risk-weighted assets; financial education

================================================================================
                           1. INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

Risk management in commercial banking rests on quantifying three interacting
exposures: borrowers may default (credit risk), funding may evaporate faster
than assets can be monetized (liquidity risk), and rate-sensitive positions
may lose value when yields shift (market risk, here focused on parallel rate
shocks and duration). Post-crisis regulation formalized stress testing and
liquidity standards; practitioners use complex engines while students often
learn formulas in isolation.

This thesis presents a unified, formula-driven teaching tool—the Bank Risk
Management Dashboard—and specifies its mathematics in alignment with the
reference implementation (TypeScript, React, Vite). Objectives:

  (1) Encode recognizable steady-state metrics (expected loss, liquidity ratio,
      duration-based loss) in one reproducible artifact.

  (2) Demonstrate scenario layering through explicit stress adjustments.

  (3) Contrast top-down portfolio default rates with bottom-up segment PDs via
      an auditable scorecard styled like interpretable ML credit models.

  (4) Document limitations so users do not confuse pedagogy with regulatory
      compliance.

Organization: Chapter 2 reviews practice; Chapter 3 gives full model math;
Chapter 4 covers software; Chapter 5 validates numerically; Chapter 6 discusses
model risk; Chapter 7 concludes.

================================================================================
              2. LITERATURE AND INSTITUTIONAL PRACTICE
================================================================================

2.1 Credit risk: PD, LGD, and EAD
----------------------------------

Industry and regulatory credit risk frameworks express expected loss as:

  EL = EAD x PD x LGD

The dashboard's classic module maps total loans to EAD, default rate (%) to
PD, and (1 - recovery rate/100) to LGD:

  EL = L x (d/100) x (1 - rho/100)

Ordinal risk bands classify adjusted default rate d': Low if d' <= 2%;
Medium if 2 < d' <= 5%; High if d' > 5%. This parallels rating-tier thinking
without simulating migration matrices or credit value adjustment.

2.2 Liquidity risk metrics and Basel III
-----------------------------------------

Basel III defines Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) and Net Stable Funding Ratio
(NSFR) with precise asset and liability categories. The dashboard uses a
simplified liquidity ratio lambda = C/D and a stress withdrawal W = D*(w/100),
with remaining cash R = C - W. Negative R triggers a bank-run warning—a
binary liquidity failure flag for teaching, not LCR reporting.

2.3 Market risk and duration
----------------------------

For small parallel yield shifts, the proportional price change of a
fixed-income position is approximated by:

  delta_V ≈ -D x V x delta_y

The implementation uses loss as a positive number:

  Loss_market = P x delta x (r/100)

where r is in percentage points. A chart sweeps r in {1, 2, 3, 4} percent to
show linear scaling—useful for introducing convexity limitations later.

2.4 Stress testing and scenario design
--------------------------------------

Stress tests apply conditional shocks to inputs. The dashboard uses binary
indicators with transparent transformations: d' = 1.8d (recession), w' = 2w
(bank run), r' = r + 2 (rate shock). These are design parameters, not
historical calibrations. Users may combine stresses to explore joint scenarios;
correlation is not modeled endogenously.

2.5 Interpretable credit scoring and model risk
-----------------------------------------------

Banks increasingly use statistical models (logistic regression, gradient
boosting, neural nets) for PD estimation. Model risk management (SR 11-7 and
international analogues) requires documentation, validation, and challenger
models. The AI Credit Default Engine is deterministic: fixed weights, sigmoid
link, no training data. It teaches structure—features, linear index, bounded
PD—without opaque estimation, supporting assignments that require hand
replication of PD from stated formulas.

                        3. MODEL SPECIFICATION
================================================================================

This section matches: src/models/riskEngine.ts (classic),

3.1 Notation and state vector
------------------------------

  L     Total loans
  d     Default rate (%)
  rho   Recovery rate (%)
  D     Total deposits
  C     Cash reserves
  w     Withdrawal shock (% of deposits)
  r     Interest rate change (% points)
  P     Loan portfolio size (rate-sensitive)
  delta Duration

3.2 Stress adjustments
----------------------

Let I_rec, I_run, I_rate in {0,1}:

  d' = 1.8*d  if I_rec=1, else d
  w' = 2*w    if I_run=1, else w
  r' = r+2    if I_rate=1, else r

3.3 Credit risk module
----------------------

  EL = L * (d'/100) * (1 - rho/100)

Twelve-month default-rate chart: d' plus sine-based drift and small random
component, clipped to [0.2, 15] percent. Randomness on re-render is illustrative
only—not Monte Carlo.

3.4 Liquidity risk module
-------------------------

  lambda = C / D
  W = D * (w'/100)
  R = C - W
  Bank run flag: R < 0

3.5 Market risk module
----------------------

  Loss_market = P * delta * (r'/100)

Chart: Loss_k = P * delta * (k/100) for k in {1,2,3,4}.

3.6 Aggregate metrics
---------------------

  T = EL + Loss_market + max(0, -R)

  S = min(100, max(0, (d'/12)*35 + (1-lambda)*30 + (r'/10)*35))

Only liquidity shortfalls (R < 0) add to T via |R|; surplus cash does not
offset credit and market loss in T.

3.7 AI credit default model (segment level)
-------------------------------------------

Each segment i has: income category g_i in {low, medium, high}; DTI_i (%);
credit score CS_i in [300,850]; interest rate IR_i (%); loan amount A_i;
employment stability E_i in [0,1].

Normalized features:
  CS_hat_i = (850 - CS_i) / (850 - 300)   [weak-credit proxy]
  DTI_hat_i = min(DTI_i, 100) / 100
  IR_hat_i = min(IR_i, 40) / 100
  E_hat_i = E_i clamped to [0,1]

Income bias b(g): +0.3 low, +0.1 medium, -0.2 high.

Linear score z_i:
  z_i = -0.4*CS_hat_i + 0.3*DTI_hat_i + 0.2*IR_hat_i * m_infl
        + b(g_i) - 0.3*E_eff_i

where m_infl = 1.5 if inflation shock active else 1;
E_eff_i = E_i/2 if job market crash else E_i.

  PD_i_raw = 1 / (1 + exp(-z_i))
  PD_i = min(1, 1.3 * PD_i_raw) if recession (AI) active; else PD_i_raw

Risk labels: Low PD < 0.10; Medium 0.10 <= PD < 0.30; High PD >= 0.30.
Amount A_i does not enter z_i; used in portfolio sums only.

3.8 Portfolio aggregation and toy RWA
-------------------------------------

  LGD = 1 - rho/100

  PD_bar = (1/n) * sum_i PD_i
  V_def = sum_i A_i * PD_i
  EL_AI = sum_i A_i * PD_i * LGD
  RWA_toy = sum_i A_i * PD_i * 1.2

High-risk count: segments with PD_i >= 0.30. Confidence label is heuristic from
average credit score and employment—not a statistical confidence interval.

3.9 What-if sliders
-------------------

Additive adjustments to DTI, credit score, and interest rate apply for scoring
only without mutating stored segment rows—supporting sensitivity analysis in
class.

================================================================================
              4. IMPLEMENTATION AND SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
================================================================================

Stack: React, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind CSS, Recharts. No backend.

Layout:
  - ControlPanel: inputs, classic stress toggles, AI Risk Mode switch, exports
  - RiskCards, RiskIndicator: KPIs
  - CreditRiskChart, LiquidityChart, MarketRiskChart: classic Recharts views
  - AICreditPanel, AICreditSummary, AIPDHistogram, AIForecastTrend, RiskHeatmap

Computation:
  runRiskEngine(state) → classic metrics (useMemo in App.tsx)
  runAIPortfolioRisk(loans, aiStress, recoveryRate) → AI aggregates

Exports:
  JSON: full engine output
  CSV: headline metrics; AI rows when AI mode on
  Loan PD CSV: id, pd, classification, loanAmount, linearScore per segment

Development server: vite.config.ts may set host 0.0.0.0 for LAN demos.

Separation of concerns: pure functions for credit, liquidity, market; AI feature
engine and default model isolated; simulation layer for portfolio aggregation.

================================================================================
                 5. NUMERICAL SCENARIOS AND VALIDATION
================================================================================

Students should replicate these checks against on-screen KPIs.

5.1 Credit expected loss
-------------------------
L = 100,000,000; d = 4%; rho = 50%.
  EL = 100,000,000 * 0.04 * 0.5 = 2,000,000
  Risk level: Medium (4% in (2,5])
Recession: d' = 7.2%; EL = 3,600,000; risk → High.

5.2 Liquidity
-------------
D = 120,000,000; C = 10,000,000; w = 15%.
  W = 18,000,000; R = -8,000,000; lambda ≈ 8.33%
  Bank run mode: w' = 30%; W = 36,000,000; deeper shortfall.

5.3 Market loss
---------------
r = 2%; P = 80,000,000; delta = 4.
  Loss_market = 80,000,000 * 4 * 0.02 = 6,400,000
Rate shock: r' = 4%; loss doubles to 12,800,000.

5.4 Composite
-------------
T includes max(0, -R) when liquidity fails. S moves with d', lambda, r'.

5.5 AI monotonicity and stress
------------------------------
Raise CS → lower PD_i; raise DTI or IR → higher PD_i (ceteris paribus).
Recession (AI): PD scaled by 1.3. Compare d'/100 to PD_bar for top-down vs
bottom-up gap. Export loan PD CSV; verify PD_i = 1/(1+exp(-z_i)) by hand.

5.6 Validation status
---------------------
Validation is deterministic replication, not back-testing. No out-of-sample
ROC, no calibration to default data. Appropriate for a master's pedagogical
artifact; insufficient for model approval in a regulated bank.

================================================================================
            6. MODEL RISK, LIMITATIONS, AND EXTENSIONS
================================================================================

Limitations (must be stated in any submission):

  1. Single-period static inputs; no multi-period path or mark-to-market
     dynamics beyond duration linearization.
  2. No correlation model between credit, liquidity, and rates.
  3. Credit chart randomness is not a Monte Carlo engine.
  4. No Basel RWA formulas by asset class; RWA_toy = A*PD*1.2 only.
  5. Scalar duration; no convexity, prepayment, or basis risk.
  6. Stress multipliers are arbitrary teaching knobs.
  7. AI weights fixed; no estimation, no PD term structure; confidence label
     is heuristic.

Model risk lessons: Students should articulate what validation would require in
production (data quality, population stability, stress calibration, governance).

Extensions for finance thesis follow-on work:
  - Second-order convexity term in rate loss
  - Correlated scenario draws with documented correlation matrix
  - Minimal balance sheet with equity and capital ratio
  - Calibration exercise on synthetic loan tape
  - Integration with external PD/LGD data feeds

Classroom: GUIDE.md test flows; PAPER.md for written assignments; AI_MODE.md
for scorecard depth.

================================================================================
                           7. CONCLUSION
================================================================================

This finance thesis specified an integrated pedagogical framework for credit,
liquidity, and market risk measurement, implemented in the Bank Risk Management
Dashboard. The classic engine delivers expected loss, liquidity stress, and
duration-based rate loss with transparent stress toggles and aggregate indices.
The AI module demonstrates segment-level PD construction, portfolio aggregation,
and traditional-versus-AI default-rate comparison without black-box machine
learning.

The artifact helps finance students see equations, change assumptions instantly,
and articulate gaps versus institutional systems. It is intentionally simple
for transparency; regulatory and enterprise use would require the extensions
listed in Chapter 6.
